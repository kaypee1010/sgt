## If Asked: "What is intelligent consumption monitoring?"
*(Prepared answer — only use if someone asks about it directly.)*

> "Think of anything that gets used up over time — a medicine bottle, a gas cylinder, a patient's IV bag. Normally, someone just checks it by hand and refills it when it runs out. My framework watches simple signals — how it looks, its temperature, how it's being handled — to predict when it's about to run out, before it becomes a problem. It isn't built for one product — it works for anything that gets 'consumed.' The medical version is the real opportunity: applying it to hospital supplies or patient consumables, which is exactly the kind of cross-department work I'd want to do with SGT's medical faculty. It's co-authored with faculty from NIET and Chandigarh University, so it's already cross-institutional."

## If Asked: "What's different about the toll fraud-detection idea?"

> "Today's toll systems check two signals — the RFID tag and the number-plate camera — and if they don't match, it just throws a flag: fraud or not. But cameras misread plates all the time, especially at night or at speed, so a lot of those flags are just noise, not fraud. My approach gives every reading a confidence score instead of a flat flag, and specifically tests how well that holds up as the sensors get worse. That's the actual contribution — nobody's tested it that way in this exact setting."

## If Asked: "What's the idea behind the agriculture work?"

> "Most rice farms today water on a fixed rule — every so many days, or when soil moisture drops below one number — regardless of weather or how the crop's actually doing. My idea is a system that decides when to irrigate based on real-time soil and weather data instead of a fixed calendar. The goal is real water savings without hurting yield."

## If Asked: "What factors did the health-risk model consider?"

> "It looks across five groups of symptoms — mood and behaviour, like sleep changes or racing thoughts; physical symptoms; anxiety signs; trauma-related symptoms; and other conditions that often occur alongside it. The mood-and-behaviour group mattered most — a reduced need for sleep and a sudden jump in goal-directed activity were the strongest signals. It's built to flag early risk, not to diagnose."

## Notes to Self — Sharper Novelty Framing
*(Not for the slides or the script — just so the real answer is in your head if someone probes.)*

**Toll / Security & Fraud Detection**
"Confidence score instead of a flag" alone is weak — a granted US patent already covers a confidence-threshold mechanism for toll ID, so that framing sits close to prior art. The actual defensible claim: testing how well confidence-aware detection **holds up as the evidence itself degrades** — RFID dropout, poor plate visibility, missing modality — which nobody's done in this exact setting. This is also why simulated data is the right tool here, not a weakness: you can't ethically corrupt real toll transactions to test degradation. Lead with robustness-under-bad-data, not with "confidence vs. flag."

**Agriculture AI (AWD)**
"Adaptive irrigation using real-time soil and weather data" is standard IoT ag-tech — not novel on its own. The actual gap: a **closed-loop reinforcement-learning policy** for AWD, with a **multi-objective reward** balancing yield, water use, and methane emissions *simultaneously*. Existing AWD work uses fixed thresholds, predicts without deciding, or optimizes one objective at a time — nobody combines RL-driven decisions with all three objectives together for AWD specifically. Lead with "the system learns the policy," not "it uses real-time data."

**Intelligent Consumption Monitoring — where do the signals come from?**
The framework doesn't assume instrumentation already exists — it specifies how to generate it with cheap, generic sensing bolted onto whatever's being consumed:
- Visual — periodic camera images, read for fill-level, wear, discoloration
- Thermal — a low-cost IR sensor (useful for things like gas cylinders, where temperature shifts as contents deplete)
- Physical — a weight/pressure sensor, measuring literal mass loss
- Behavioural — usage logging (taps opened, drawer accessed, dispenser triggered)

The generalization is in the modelling layer: swap the 1–2 signal types that make sense for a given item, and the same prediction engine underneath doesn't care what's actually being consumed.
