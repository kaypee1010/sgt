About the Course

Generative AI has moved from a technical curiosity to a working tool in every profession. Students already reach for it while writing, revising, analysing and preparing. What they do not get is instruction: how these systems actually work, how to ask for something precisely, and how to tell when the answer is wrong.

AI Foundations closes that gap. It covers how predictive and generative systems work, prompting as a professional skill, verification of AI output, and responsible use under Indian law. The gap it addresses is not awareness — students already have the tools — it is supervised skill. That is why verification is taught as a skill and assessed as one, rather than mentioned as good advice.

The course is laboratory-dominant by design: 30 practical hours against 15 lecture hours, because a student learns prompting and verification by doing them. It is written to sit inside the UGC Curriculum and Credit Framework (Value-Added Courses) and the UNESCO AI Competency Framework for Students (2024).

Applied AI / Future Track (1 credit)

After the common foundation, each School adds one credit of practical work, designed by its Board of Studies from a common template and finalised with the Dean and Head of Department. Students work with AI code generation openly: they define the problem in a strict prompt structure, then spend their effort on debugging, evaluating and verifying the result, so a management or life-science student can build a small working application.

Instructor Bio

Kamal Preet Singh, AI and ML Expert — under the guidance of Dr. Shalini Kapoor, Director (R&D), SGT University
Offered by
AI-SETU, the SGT Centre for AI Excellence (Skills, Ethics and Technology Upskilling), with the Department of Computer Science (AI and ML), SOET
Who teaches
AI-SETU designs and owns the syllabus, lecture notes, faculty slide decks, the central question bank and assessment. Delivery is hub-and-spoke: one nominated faculty member per School, certified through the AI-SETU boot camp, leads the laboratory sessions and the Future Track locally, supported by postgraduate demonstrators from SOET AI and ML programmes.
How sessions run
  • Every session opens with a live AI success or failure, then moves to the concept
  • About 25% interactive lecture, 50% hands-on and group work, 25% self-directed learning
  • Case-based learning on real incidents; the AI tribunal role play; predict-the-output exercises
  • Think-pair-share and peer critique of prompts; an exit ticket every session
