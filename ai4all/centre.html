Proposal for establishment

Proposal for establishment of AI-SETU: SGT Centre for AI Literacy & Assessment

One central Centre that builds Ai4All once, runs synchronised assessments for all 19 Schools, delivers the teaching content, and trains the faculty who teach it.

  • 1Centre, reporting to the Vice-Chancellor
  • 19SETU Leads, one per School
  • 4functions: S, E, T, U
  • 6–7core staff, mostly by secondment

Decision requested

Approval in principle is sought to:

  1. Establish AI-SETU, the SGT Centre for AI Literacy & Assessment, reporting to the Vice-Chancellor, with the mandate below.
  2. Appoint a Head and an Academic Lead by secondment from existing faculty, with teaching relief, from October 2026.
  3. Nominate one SETU Lead in each of the 19 Schools.
  4. Allocate existing space for an office, a 30-seat training room and a student AI clinic, and approve procurement of one institutional AI tool.

This Centre runs the programme described in the Ai4All proposal and the course proposal.

1. Why a Centre is needed

Nineteen Schools cannot each build and maintain an AI course, a question bank, lab material, an exam standard and faculty training. If they try, SGT ends up with nineteen different versions of the same subject, duplicated effort, and no single claim about what its graduates can do. A Centre does the common work once, at one standard, and leaves each School in control of the part only it can write: its own Applied AI course.

Without a Centre

  • 19 syllabi drifting apart
  • Different exams and marking standards
  • Every School builds its own slides and labs
  • No control over which AI tools touch student data

With AI-SETU

  • One master syllabus, one question bank
  • Same paper, same window, same rubric in every School
  • One ready-to-teach kit, updated yearly
  • One approved tool and one DPDP-aligned data rule

2. Name

AI-SETU, SGT Centre for AI Literacy & Assessment. Setu means bridge: a bridge between AI and every discipline at SGT. The four letters name its four functions: Standards, Evaluation, Teaching, Upskilling. Alternatives, if preferred: Pragya Centre for AI Literacy (pragya: discernment) or Bodhi AI Literacy Hub.

3. Mandate: four functions

S

Standards

Master Tier 1 syllabus and outcomes; the Tier 2 template; the Tier 3 track design; draft AI-use policy for IQAC; annual refresh.

E

Evaluation

Central question and task bank; synchronised quizzes and common end-term practical; rubrics; moderation; attainment analytics.

T

Teaching

Lecture decks, lab sheets, facilitator guides, recorded walkthroughs, student packs, all delivered through the LMS.

U

Upskilling

Faculty Tracks A, B and C with certification; approved AI tools and data rules; student AI clinic.

What the Centre will not do

  • Write or approve Schools' Tier 2 content. Boards of Studies own that.
  • Teach every batch itself. Delivery stays with certified faculty in each School.
  • Act as a parallel academic authority. Courses are still approved by the Boards of Studies and Academic Council.
  • Handle identifiable patient, client or student data. It sets the rule; it does not process the data.

4. Synchronised assessment system

The Centre's most important job: every student in every School is assessed to the same standard, at the same time, and the University can report attainment with evidence.

  1. WriteFaculty item-writers draft questions and verification tasks, including AI outputs with planted errors.
  2. Review and tagThe Assessment Officer reviews each item and tags it to a learning outcome and a Bloom level.
  3. BankItems enter an access-controlled bank on the LMS with version history and audit logs.
  4. AssembleQuizzes and the end-term practical are built from the bank, with equivalent variants so that Schools sitting at different times get different but equal papers.
  5. DeliverAll Schools sit in the same published window on the LMS.
  6. Mark and moderateObjective items are auto-marked; practicals use common rubrics; a moderation sample from every School is re-marked centrally.
  7. ReportOutcome attainment by School, programme and outcome goes to IQAC each semester.
  8. ImproveItems that are too easy, too hard or unclear are revised or retired before the next cycle.

8. Space, platform and facilities

Space (existing, re-purposed)

  • Office with 4–6 workstations
  • 30-seat computer training room for faculty tracks
  • Student AI clinic, 12–15 seats
  • Small recording corner for short videos
  • Shared use of existing School labs; no new building, no GPUs

Digital platform

  • LMS course shell cloned to every School
  • Question bank with tagging, variants and audit logs
  • Attainment dashboard for IQAC
  • One institutional AI assistant with data-protection terms
  • Cloud notebook sandbox for Tier 3

9. Phased establishment

  1. October–December 2026: establishApprove and name the Centre; second the Head and Academic Lead; constitute the Advisory Board; draft the Tier 1 syllabus, question bank and blueprint.
  2. December 2026–February 2027: staff and equipFill remaining roles; fit out rooms; procure the AI tool; approve the data SOP; run Track A for all faculty and Track B for pilot facilitators.
  3. January–May 2027: run the pilotDeliver the kit and synchronised assessments in five Schools; moderate the first cycle.
  4. June–July 2027: reviewPublish the first annual report; revise content and items; finalise the Tier 2 template.
  5. 2027-28 onwards: full operationAll 19 SETU Leads in post; university-wide Tier 1 from January 2028; Tier 2 as cohorts reach it.

11. How the Centre's own performance is measured

  • Master kit and question bank ready before each semester starts.
  • Every Tier 1 facilitator certified before teaching (Track B coverage 100%).
  • Common assessments held in the same window in every School; moderation completed within the results timeline.
  • Semester attainment report to IQAC on time, by School and outcome.
  • All 19 Tier 2 courses approved by their Boards of Studies within the planned window.
  • Zero incidents of personal data entered into non-approved AI tools in teaching.
  • Faculty and student satisfaction with Centre support at 4 out of 5 or higher.
