2. Executive summary

AI Foundations is a common, 2-credit, introductory course for every student in the 19 non-engineering Schools. It teaches how AI works, how to instruct it clearly, how to verify its output, and how to use it lawfully and ethically. It is identical in every School, taught by AI-SETU-certified faculty using one central content kit, question bank and common end-term practical. It is followed later by a 2-credit Applied AI course written by each School on a common template (Annexure XI). Implementation starts with a five-School pilot in January 2027 and a university-wide launch for batches admitted in 2027-28.

3. Background and rationale

3.1 Academic context

The UGC Curriculum and Credit Framework (2022) lists "Digital and technological solutions", naming AI, machine learning and deep learning, among the Value-Added Courses common to all UG students. It also lists digital and technological skills as a graduate attribute. UNESCO's AI Competency Framework for Students (2024) defines AI competence across a human-centred mindset, ethics, techniques and applications, and system design.

3.2 Industry context

Generative and predictive AI are now routine in documentation, analysis, customer service, diagnostics support, legal research and media. The professions expect graduates to use these tools while keeping human accountability, as India's AI Governance Guidelines (MeitY, 2025) and the ICMR guidelines on AI in healthcare (2023) make explicit.

3.3 Student context

Students already use consumer AI tools. Evidence shows that unguided use can reduce independent learning (Bastani et al., PNAS 2025) and that AI tools fabricate academic references (Walters & Wilder, Scientific Reports 2023). From 2026-27, schools across India begin teaching AI and computational thinking, so incoming students will increasingly arrive with some exposure and need a university-level next step.

3.4 Institutional context

A single standard across 19 Schools supports NAAC and NIRF positioning, prepares SGT for the DPDP Rules taking full effect on 14 May 2027, and creates a base for interdisciplinary AI research and industry engagement.

6. Course aim

To enable every student to use AI tools effectively, verify their outputs rigorously, and apply them responsibly and lawfully in academic and professional work.

7. Course objectives

  1. Build a correct, non-technical understanding of how predictive and generative AI systems work.
  2. Develop the skill of specifying tasks to AI through clear, structured and iterative prompts.
  3. Establish verification of AI claims and citations as a routine, assessed practice.
  4. Develop judgement on privacy, confidentiality, copyright, integrity and disclosure.
  5. Enable students to design human-checked AI workflows for real tasks.

Aligned with the SGT Faculty Handbook on Facilitation and Andragogic Techniques: about 25% interactive lecture, 50% student-led hands-on and group work, 25% self-directed learning.

11. Practical and hands-on component

  • Prompt log: the same task improved over three versions, with reasons.
  • No-code image classifier (Teachable Machine or similar) on everyday objects, to see how training data shapes results.
  • "Find the fabrication": audit AI-written briefs with planted false claims and fake references.
  • Citation audit using library databases and DOI lookup.
  • Bias probe: same question framed for different groups; compare answers.
  • With-AI vs without-AI comparison on a study or document task.
  • Structured-output tasks: tables, checklists, rubrics.
  • Capstone: design, test and present a human-checked AI workflow for a campus or general workplace task.

All practical data is discipline-neutral and non-personal, so the same labs run in every School.

14. Programme and curriculum mapping

  • Prerequisite: none.
  • Three-tier position: this course is Tier 1 (AI Literacy). Tier 2 is the School's Applied AI course; Tier 3 is Core AI/ML for technical tracks (Annexure XIII).
  • Subsequent course: the School's Applied AI course (2 credits, mid-programme), for which AI Foundations is the prerequisite (Annexure XI).
  • Programme outcomes: strongest contribution to digital literacy, critical thinking and ethics; moderate to communication, research skills and self-directed learning (Annexure II).
  • Interdisciplinary use: identical course for all 19 Schools; mixed-School batches possible.
  • Regulated programmes: in hours-based programmes (MBBS, BDS, BAMS) the course runs as a certified value-added course unless the regulator permits credit; the Applied AI component uses regulator slots (NMC electives, INC informatics course, NCISM electives, PCI electives).
  • Duplication: none with discipline courses; complements PCI Computer Applications in Pharmacy (so B.Pharm takes it in Semester III) and ICAR AI content.

16. Industry relevance

  • Skills employers use: drafting and summarising, data assistance, documentation, research support, all with checking.
  • Applications by field: radiograph AI in dentistry, clinical decision support, legal research, audit analytics, revenue management, fact-checking (taken further in Applied AI, Annexure XI).
  • Job relevance: AI-assisted roles in every profession, with human accountability intact.
  • Engagement plan: guest sessions and case material from employers in the capstone; internship supervisors rate AI readiness.
  • Industry feedback: to be collected during the pilot (Annexure VI).

17. Infrastructure and technology requirements

Hardware

  • Existing computer labs, one seat per student or per pair
  • Projector in every classroom
  • Stable campus internet; BYOD allowed
  • No GPU at any level

Software and platforms

  • One institutionally approved generative AI assistant
  • University LMS for content, quizzes and the question bank
  • Browser-based no-code tools (e.g. Teachable Machine) and spreadsheets
  • Library database access
  • Standard sandbox: the same tool set in every School; one cloud notebook environment (e.g. Google Colab) for Tier 3

Licensing

  • Institutional AI licence: per user, sized to active cohorts, priced by quote
  • Free and open-source alternatives for all lab tools
  • No per-student cost for lab software

Data, privacy and security

  • No personal, patient, client or student-identifiable data in any AI tool
  • Approved tool must not train on user inputs
  • DPDP-aligned SOP in place before 14 May 2027
  • Question bank in access-controlled storage with audit logs

19. Student eligibility and intake

  • Eligible programmes: all UG and PG programmes of the 19 Schools.
  • Semester: II for UG; I for PG and graduate-entry; III for B.Pharm.
  • Prerequisites: none.
  • Intake: whole cohort; batches of 40–50 per facilitator, minimum batch 20.
  • Open elective use: SOET students and existing batches may take it as a non-credit certificate course.

20. Certification and academic credit

  • Credit-bearing: 2 credits, graded, recorded on the transcript and in the Academic Bank of Credits.
  • Hours-based regulated programmes: university certificate and transcript entry until credit treatment is confirmed.
  • Digital badge "SGT AI Literate" on passing; a second badge "SGT AI Professional ([discipline])" after Applied AI.

22. Monitoring and evaluation

  • Student feedback each semester (target relevance 4/5 or higher).
  • CLO attainment computed from tagged question-bank items and rubrics.
  • Pre/post verification test: target gain of 30 percentage points in detecting planted errors.
  • Calibration: how closely students' confidence matches their accuracy on verification tasks.
  • Project and viva quality on a moderated rubric.
  • Internship supervisor and employer feedback.
  • Annual review of syllabus, tool list and question bank; report to IQAC.

25. Conclusion and approval requested

Need: students use AI without training, and SGT has no common AI standard. Solution: a common 2-credit AI Foundations course plus School-owned Applied AI, run by AI-SETU. Benefits: verified AI literacy for every graduate and a clear institutional signature. Feasibility: existing labs, no capital-heavy items, a pilot first. Resources: AI-SETU core team, AI licence, LMS set-up.

The Board of Studies and Academic Council are requested to approve AI Foundations (AI-UG-CORE-101) as a 2-credit compulsory Value-Added Course for all programmes of the 19 Schools, the Applied AI template, and the January 2027 pilot.

Annexure XI: Tier 2, Applied AI framework
