Proposal for approval in principle

Proposal for introduction of AI Foundations: a university-wide AI literacy course

Ai4All: an AI course for every student, an Applied AI course in every School, and the AI-SETU Centre to run them at one standard.

  • 19Schools covered
  • 3tiers of AI learning
  • 2 + 2credits (Tier 1 + Tier 2)
  • Jan 2027five-School pilot

Decision requested

Approval in principle is sought for:

  1. Tier 1: developing and introducing AI Foundations, a common 2-credit course for every student in the 19 Schools.
  2. Tier 2: a discipline-specific Applied AI course (2 credits) in each School, built on one common template; Tier 3 Core AI/ML offered as an elective or minor.
  3. Establishing AI-SETU, the SGT Centre for AI Literacy & Assessment, to own the content, question banks, assessments and faculty training centrally.
  4. A five-School pilot from January 2027.

After approval in principle, the detailed curriculum, academic structure and implementation documents will be placed before the Boards of Studies, Academic Council and IQAC. The detailed academic proposal is already prepared (Course proposal).

1. Executive summary

AI is now a working tool in every profession our graduates enter, from dental radiography and legal research to audit, nursing documentation and journalism. Most SGT students use AI tools already, but nobody teaches them to check what these tools say, protect confidential data, or use AI without weakening their own learning.

This proposal builds AI into the 19 Schools in three tiers. Tier 1, AI Literacy: one common 2-credit course, AI Foundations, in Semester II for every student, covering how AI and data work, how to instruct AI, how to verify its output and how to use it responsibly. Tier 2, Applied AI: a 2-credit course in each student's own discipline, placed mid-programme. Tier 3, Core AI/ML: optional Python-based machine learning for students on technical tracks. All three are run through AI-SETU, a central Centre that builds the lectures, lab sheets, question banks and common exams once, and trains faculty to deliver them in every School.

Who it is for

All UG and PG students in the 19 Schools, starting with batches admitted in 2027-28, after a five-School pilot in January 2027.

What students gain

Graduates who can use AI productively, catch its errors, keep data safe, and apply it to real tasks in their profession.

2. Background and context

  • National direction. NEP 2020 and the UGC credit framework name AI among the "Digital and technological solutions" courses to be offered to all UG students, and give them a place in the Value-Added Course basket.
  • Peer institutions have moved. IIT Delhi has made at least one AI or machine learning course compulsory in every UG and PG programme. International universities now run AI literacy for all students.
  • Incoming students will expect it. The Ministry of Education is introducing AI and computational thinking in schools from 2026-27.
  • Regulators have made room. NMC lists AI as an elective area for MBBS; INC, PCI and ICAR curricula already carry informatics or AI content; India's AI Governance Guidelines (2025) and the DPDP Rules set clear expectations on responsible use and data protection.

3. Need for the proposed course

3.1 Students

Students use AI without training. Research shows unguided use can lower independent performance (a 17% drop once AI was removed, in a study of nearly 1,000 students), and AI tools regularly invent references. Students need the skill of checking, not just access.

3.2 Industry and professions

Employers expect graduates who can work with AI tools safely: drafting, analysis, documentation and decision support, with a clear sense of where professional judgement must take over. Health and law add strict duties on confidentiality and accountability.

3.3 SGT University

One university-wide standard gives SGT a clear, defensible claim for NAAC, NIRF and admissions: every SGT graduate is AI-literate and AI-responsible. The Applied AI layer opens interdisciplinary teaching, research projects and industry partnerships in all 19 Schools, and the DPDP deadline of May 2027 makes a university AI data policy necessary anyway.

4. Problem or gap being addressed

What exists todayWhat is missing

Scattered informatics or computer courses in some regulated programmesNo common standard, no verification skills, no responsible-use training
Students using consumer AI tools on their ownNo university rules on disclosure or on keeping patient, client and student data out of AI tools

The AI-SETU Centre

Setu means bridge: a bridge between AI and every discipline at SGT. Nineteen Schools cannot each build and maintain their own AI course, question bank and faculty training. AI-SETU does the common work once and lets each School own only what is truly its own. Its four functions give the name:

S

Standards

One master syllabus, the Applied AI template, the AI-use policy, and an annual refresh.

E

Evaluation

Centralised, synchronised assessments: a common question and task bank, common end-term practical, rubrics and moderation.

T

Teaching

Ready-to-teach lecture decks, lab sheets, facilitator guides and student packs, delivered through the LMS.

U

Upskilling

Faculty training and certification, approved AI tools and data rules, and an outcomes dashboard for IQAC.

One trained SETU Lead in each School acts as the spoke: timetabling, Applied AI design with the Board of Studies, and feedback to the Centre.

6. Vision and objectives

Vision: every SGT graduate uses AI confidently, checks it rigorously, and applies it responsibly in their profession.

  1. Build a common AI literacy standard across all 19 Schools.
  2. Make verification of AI output a graded, demonstrable skill.
  3. Teach responsible, lawful use: privacy, confidentiality, copyright and academic integrity.
  4. Connect AI to each discipline's real professional tasks.
  5. Run the initiative centrally, so quality is consistent and cost stays low.

7. What students will learn and be able to do

Knowledge

How AI, machine learning and generative AI work in plain language; why AI gives confident wrong answers.

Practical skills

Writing clear, structured prompts; using AI for study, documents, data and presentations.

Critical evaluation

Verifying claims and citations; spotting bias and fabricated sources.

Responsible use and application

Knowing what data must never enter an AI tool; disclosing AI use; designing human-checked workflows for their own profession.

9. Teaching and learning approach

Following the SGT Faculty Handbook: 25% interactive lecture, 50% hands-on and group work, 25% guided self-study. Every session opens with a live AI success or failure as the hook. Students critique flawed AI output before creating their own, work through case studies and role plays, and finish with a project. Some tasks are deliberately done without AI, so students see the difference in their own learning.

10. Distinctiveness and value proposition

  • Verification at the centre. Most AI courses teach using AI; this one grades the ability to catch AI's mistakes.
  • Three tiers. A common foundation, a professional course in every discipline, and a coding track for those who want it.
  • Centrally run, synchronised. Same content, same exam, same standard in all 19 Schools through AI-SETU.
  • Built to last. The syllabus names skills, not products, so it does not go out of date with each new AI tool.
  • A signature for SGT. "Every SGT graduate is AI-literate and AI-responsible" is a clear message for students, parents, employers and accreditors.

12. Expected impact

Students

  • Practical AI skills usable from Semester II onwards
  • Employability in AI-enabled workplaces
  • Stronger research and academic writing with honest AI use
  • A professional sense of where AI must stop

University

  • A university-wide academic innovation for NAAC and NIRF
  • Interdisciplinary teaching and research across 19 Schools
  • A base for industry partnerships and future AI minors or certificates
  • A DPDP-ready AI data policy

13. Implementation approach

  • Launch: five-School pilot in January 2027 (Commerce & Management, Law, Allied Health, Behavioural Sciences, Media), then all Schools for batches admitted in 2027-28.
  • Delivery: AI-SETU builds everything; AI-SETU-certified faculty in each School teach in batches of 40–50.
  • Digital: the university LMS hosts content, quizzes and the question bank; one university-approved AI tool with data-protection terms.
  • Standard sandbox: the same approved AI assistant, LMS and browser tools in every School, plus one cloud notebook environment for Tier 3, so no School depends on local hardware.
  • Resources: existing computer labs; no GPUs, no new building.

14. Resource and financial implications

One-time

  • AI-SETU office and a 30-seat training room (fit-out of existing space)
  • Course development: content, lab sheets, question bank (by seconded faculty)
  • LMS set-up and a small content-recording corner

Recurring

  • Institutional AI tool licence (the main cost; per-user, priced by quote)
  • AI-SETU core team of 6–7, mostly by secondment in the first two years
  • Stipends for 2–3 PG demonstrators per semester
  • Annual content and question-bank refresh

Rupee figures will be added by Finance after vendor quotes for the AI licence and LMS work. No capital-heavy items (servers, GPUs, new buildings) are needed.

16. Proposed timeline

  1. October 2026Concept approval (this proposal).
  2. October–December 2026AI-SETU set up; course content, lab sheets and question bank developed.
  3. December 2026Academic review by the Boards of Studies and Academic Council.
  4. December 2026–February 2027LMS and AI tool set-up; faculty preparation and certification.
  5. January–May 2027Pilot in five Schools.
  6. June 2027–January 2028Review and revision; every School builds its Tier 2 course.
  7. From January 2028University-wide launch for batches admitted in 2027-28.

17. Success indicators

  • Participation and completion: every enrolled student in the 19 Schools completes AI Foundations.
  • Learning outcomes: at least 75% of students reach "proficient" on the verification rubric.
  • Skill gain: detection of fake citations improves by at least 30 percentage points from start to end.
  • Responsible use: at least 80% correctly judge allowed and disallowed AI uses in a scenario test.
  • Feedback: student and faculty relevance rating of 4 out of 5 or higher.
  • Applied AI projects scored against a moderated rubric; employer and industry feedback from internships.
