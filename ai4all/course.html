About the course

AI tools can draft, summarise, analyse and explain in seconds, but they also invent facts, cite papers that do not exist, and reflect hidden biases. This course teaches you to get real value from AI while staying in charge of the result.

You will learn how today's AI systems produce their answers, practise writing clear instructions, and build the habit that matters most: checking. Every week has a hands-on lab. You finish by designing an AI workflow for a real task, with clear points where a person checks the work and where AI should not be used at all.

The course is the same for every student at SGT, whatever your School. After it, your own School's Applied AI course shows how AI is used in your profession.

AI
Try it: spot the fabricationA chatbot gave these three answers. Which would you trust?

  • "Under the UGC credit framework, one practicum credit equals about 30 hours of engagement in a semester."

    Correct. You can check this in the UGC Curriculum and Credit Framework (2022): 2 hours a week over 15 weeks.

  • "This is well established: see Sharma & Rao (2021), Journal of Indian AI Pedagogy, 14(2), 45–61."

    This reference was invented for this exercise, exactly the way chatbots invent them. A real citation can be found in a library database or by its DOI. Always check before you cite.

  • "A chatbot writes by repeatedly predicting a likely next word, so it can sound confident even when it is wrong."

    Correct. That is why fluent is not the same as accurate, and why this course grades checking.

Answer all three to see your score. Module 3 turns this into a habit.

Course outline

Each week: one 1-hour interactive lecture and one 2-hour lab. Tap a module to see its weeks.

1How AI and data workWeeks 1–3 · 9 h

AI, machine learning and generative AI in plain language; how data is collected and how charts can mislead; how models learn from data; how a chatbot picks its next word.

AI typesData literacyHow chatbots work
  • Week 1: What AI is and is not. Lab: set up tools; start your prompt log
  • Week 2: Predictive vs generative AI. Lab: train a no-code image classifier and watch it fail
  • Week 3: Inside a chatbot. Lab: predict the output, then test it
2Instructing AIWeeks 4–6 · 9 h

Prompt designFew-shotStructured output
  • Week 4: Anatomy of a good prompt. Lab: improve a prompt over three versions
  • Week 5: Examples and structured output. Lab: tables, checklists and rubrics
  • Week 6: Multi-step tasks. Lab: compare two AI tools on the same job
3Checking AI outputWeeks 7–9 · 9 h

HallucinationCitation auditBias
  • Week 7: Hallucination. Lab: find the fabrication
  • Week 8: Sources and citations. Lab: citation audit with library databases
  • Week 9: Bias and uncertainty. Lab: bias probe and mid-course quiz

  • Week 10: Learning with AI. Lab: no-AI checkpoint, then AI-assisted revision
  • Week 11: AI at work. Lab: same task with and without AI; compare

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to know programming?

No. Everything uses ordinary AI assistants and browser-based tools.

Can I use AI in my assignments?

Yes, within the university's green, amber and red rules, and always with a disclosure. Using AI where a task forbids it, or submitting fake citations, is misconduct.

Which AI tool will we use?

A university-approved assistant that does not train on your inputs. The course teaches skills that work on any tool.

Is my data safe?

You will never enter personal, patient or client data into any AI tool during the course. Labs use public or made-up data only.
